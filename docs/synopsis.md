# Lychkin: synopsis (contains spoilers)

*All names are working titles.*

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Genre** | Pixel-art dark fantasy story RPG, no combat |
| **Length** | About 2 hours per run |
| **Play** | Environmental puzzles and riddles, chase sequences with overworld death |
| **Tone** | Melancholy and dread from setting and sound, not horror. Jumpscares are rare and intentional |
| **Themes** | Religious trauma, love versus faith, inherited duty, the cycle that continues |
| **Inspirations** | The Witch's House, Mad Father, Fear & Hunger, Dark Souls, Undertale |
| **Endings** | Three: one default, two hidden |

## The setup

Beneath a cathedral lies **Caldris**, a being the **Lumen Order** calls a demon. The older faith the Order overwrote may have called him a god. He is bound by a **Seal** that is kept shut by the devotion and rituals of one bloodline, the **Lychkin**. The magic the distant city of **Vareth** enjoys, which the Order presents as divine blessings, is drawn from his imprisonment.

**Maren** is the Lychkin heir. She was raised from birth as a priestess and brought as a child to Hollowmere Manor to learn her duty. There she met Caldris, first as something frightening and then as the one presence that comforted her. That affection is the crack in the Seal.

## The story

Maren returns to Hollowmere Manor as its new priestess to perform the overdue Rite that renews the Seal. Caldris, too weak to leave, reaches out through shadow animals: a dog and a crow. Maren knows they are him. She never says so, and the player has to work it out from their conversations, which grow strangely tender.

She explores the manor, its grounds and the cathedral beside it, solving puzzles and riddles written in two faiths. Things warped by the leaking Seal chase her, growing stranger the deeper she descends. A young acolyte named **Edrin**, sincere and kind, helps her and keeps offering her the safe, sanctioned life. Her parent, the current Lychkin holder, waits in the cathedral. They love her, but only conditionally.

## Characters

- **Maren**: the protagonist. Devout, conflicted and in love with something her faith calls a demon.
- **Caldris**: the sealed being. Tender and tragic. Haunted by fear that she will close the door on him.
- **Edrin**: an acolyte and Maren's human helper. Represents what becomes of someone who never goes against their religious captors. Sides with the Order in every ending.
- **The parent**: loving, controlling, not a villain.
- **Wardens**: the Order's enforcers. They chase Maren inside the cathedral.
- **The Bound**: strange non-animal creatures of the old faith, imprisoned by the Order and loyal to Caldris. Very well hidden.
- **The Warped**: mindless things twisted by the leaking Seal. They are the chasers.

## The three endings

### 1. Good Ending? (the default)
Anyone who follows the story reaches this. Maren completes the Rite. In a short chase through the crypt, the shadow animals, including the crow the player has grown fond of, try to stop her, and the player reads Caldris as the threat. The crow dies because of the player's own action. The epilogue shows an older Maren teaching her child the Rite, the child already bearing the Mark. Edrin is her implied spouse. Small signs show the corruption continues, and one image points back to an early act of defiance. The "?" in the title tells the player there is more.

### 2. Bad Ending (deliberate and hidden)
The player finds hidden Bound creatures, frees them, and collects three pieces of a forbidden verse. At the Seal, Maren can choose to speak it. The verse has drifted over centuries, and one line implies she will betray Caldris. Already low on sanity from his imprisonment, he takes it as the last straw and returns corrupted. A chase runs through the entire manor with him as the pursuer. When Maren survives and shows her devotion, he shows a vulnerable physical form, and her parent kills him. Vareth's magic fails, crops wither and entities roam free. The Order falls. Edrin survives as a grieving witness. The epilogue shows a secret door in the crypt, opened and closed by a Bound creature.

### 3. Secret Ending (the hardest to find)
The player must have everything needed for the Bad Ending, and also four of five Old Faith items read correctly, which opens two hidden crypt areas. There the original wording of the verse is carved. Speaking the correct line frees Caldris uncorrupted. The Order still refuses to let him live, so Maren and Caldris choose to be sealed away together. Her Mark now powers a Seal built on love instead of obedience. It no longer leaks, the chasers stop spawning, and Vareth flourishes even more. Vareth never learns the truth, and the Order keeps its lies. Edrin becomes keeper at the sealed door, but a small detail shows him beginning to doubt the Order. It is left open whether he will break the cycle, but Maren's sacrifice has set something in motion.

## Design principles

- **The default ending needs no secret knowledge.** The other two are never prompted. The game gives no quest log and no warnings about saving, and it trusts the player to look.
- **Each ending hints at the next.** The title screen shows "Good Ending?" and a blank slot for the others.
- **A second playthrough re-reads the first.** Early lines from the crow, a childhood drawing, and the chase in the Good Ending all mean something different once you know.
- **Chases are fair.** Each is triggered by something the player did.
- **Lore is implied, not explained.** The world is bigger than what the player sees. Several short games in the same universe are planned.
